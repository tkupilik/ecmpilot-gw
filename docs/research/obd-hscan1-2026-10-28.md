## Result

The passive traffic on **standard OBD pins 6/14**, confirmed by the owner, has a strong PSA **HS.HS1** fingerprint. There are coherent chassis, BSI and electric-powertrain broadcast messages, including battery telemetry. The previous private-powertrain/eCAN DBC is not suitable for this traffic.

This establishes useful passive access at the diagnostic socket. It does **not** prove direct copper continuity to every HS1 port, transparent BSI forwarding, or the ability to issue heating/charging commands from this location. An ESP32 OBD dongle is a promising gateway form factor if command reachability and operation during vehicle sleep are subsequently demonstrated.

Input: `logs/obd_hscan1.csv`, SHA-256 `f362faeb34a69611715247b5edb1dc9a051af7910c4da2e3e0d51c91436cdc03`.

### Battery sweep consistency

`0x334` sends groups 0…26 about 20 ms apart, then pauses before the next sweep. All 108 cells are covered once per roughly one-second sweep, not once per 20 ms. The 47 complete sweeps sum to **415.551…415.980 V**, with individual cell values **3.823…3.855 V**. This agrees with `0x5C3` pack voltage of **415…416 V** and gives substantially stronger evidence than plausible individual voltages alone. See [per-cell ranges](../../dbc/opel_corsa_e_hscan1.cells.csv).

`0x3F4` sends groups 0…7 about 50 ms apart, likewise with a pause before the next cycle. Its last two payload slots in group 7 are zero-filled here. The DBC therefore exposes **54 logical slots**, with group-dependent validity. This does not establish 54 distinct installed thermistors: repeated/default fields and physical sensor numbering require confirmation.

Times are relative to the first captured frame. State labels use the source mapping `0=P, 1=R, 2=N, 3=D` and are supported by the independent BSI reverse flag.

| Relative time | Observation |

| 11.24…12.92 s | N |

| 18.14…20.12 s and 27.88…30.20 s | Two brief nonzero wheel-speed intervals while R is selected |
| 30.93…31.23 s | N |
| 31.23…37.40 s | D; BSI reverse flag already cleared |
| 32.54…35.04 s and 35.96…37.44 s | Two further nonzero wheel-speed intervals while D is selected; wheel signals return to zero |

| From 40.20 s | P; parking-lock status engaged at 40.590 s |

The source mapping decodes R before D, whereas the initial informal description listed D before R. Keep this distinction visible until the owner confirms the action order. Wheel speeds are unsigned and do not independently determine direction.

## Definitions deliberately not adopted

- `0A6`: observed DLC 2, source DLC 5. Source name and immobiliser-related content are internally inconsistent. No wheel/immobiliser decode adopted.
- `495`: DLC 4 versus source 6; `4F8`: 5 versus 8; `543`: 4 versus 6. These layouts are rejected for this capture.
- `25A`, `352`, `3FA`, `494`, `4A2`: no inherited HS.HS1 definition. Same-ID definitions from other buses are not evidence for these layouts.
- `40B`, `42B`, `44B`: source omits DLC; camera-family attribution remains provisional.
- `563`: alternates distinct-looking payloads at different phases within each second. A naive single layout produces alternating zero capacity and inconsistent gear. Kept raw; a missing multiplexer, variant or multiple transmitters remains unresolved.
- `5A3`: upstream factor **11** on cell indices yields impossible indices up to 1133. DBC uses raw factor **1**, explicitly marked candidate. Cell indices/ordering are not independently validated.
- `3F4`: an upstream last-byte temperature field lacks its group condition and would decode padding as −40 C. DBC reconstructs logical group/slot multiplexing instead of publishing that field as always valid.
- `5C1`: source calls the frame `BML_VERSION`, but its documented fields and varying payload concern temperatures. DBC uses a neutral descriptive frame name and only two plausible fields.
- `208` and inherited gearbox fields contain ICE/automatic-transmission semantics that are not safe to transplant wholesale onto this EV. For example, `208` RPM is an invalid sentinel throughout the capture. Only reviewed gear fields from other messages are adopted.
- `58E` vent temperatures are all zero-coded, numerically −40 C under the source formula. These are not evidence of actual vent temperature and remain raw.

Only `0A6`, `454`, `494`, `552` overlap the previous local powertrain DBC. Its labels `UDS_Response_454` / `UDS_Request_494` must not be carried over: here these IDs transmit fixed periodic data, with no identified ISO-TP/UDS transaction. Diagnostic addressing depends on network/context; an ID collision is not a protocol identification.

## DBC validation and remaining work

The generated [DBC](../../dbc/opel_corsa_e_hscan1.dbc) represents all 86 observed IDs: **19 messages with reviewed fields, 67 raw-only messages, 609 total signal definitions**. Definitions carry confidence/source notes. See [DBC usage and licensing](../../dbc/README.md).

Validation reloads the actual exported DBC in `cantools` 41.4.0 with strict signal bounds/overlap checks, decodes **all 75,231 frames**, compares **56,426 named decoded values** against an independent MSB-first extractor, checks **302,714 raw fallback bytes**, and verifies the 47 complete cell sweeps. This validates DBC conversion, bit ordering and multiplexing; it cannot establish every physical signal's meaning. Exact results and hashes are in [validation JSON](../../dbc/opel_corsa_e_hscan1.validation.json).

The owner reports UDS works on the same OBD pair. For the next validation capture, record UDS request/response pairs alongside broadcasts, preferably with the already working Android dashboard's ECU/DID definitions. Compare SOC, pack voltage, individual/min/max cell voltages, battery/inverter temperatures, SOH and odometer. Respect units, invalid sentinels, sampling delay and the distinction between displayed SOC and BMS SOC. No diagnostic requests were sent as part of this analysis.

To qualify the OBD dongle architecture, capture the effect of a real heating/charging action and identify the command path and response; receiving status on this pair does not prove commands will be accepted from it. Wake-up, sleep/current consumption and availability of this traffic after READY ends also remain open. Any later firmware decoding should publish validated signals into varstore; application control continues to consume varstore in its separate process.
